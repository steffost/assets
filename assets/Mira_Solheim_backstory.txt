The hint suggests something that pulses and hovers, a living floater. Looking at the world bible, I find rich material. The Pearl is the biosphere at the top of Ombra Prime, lush and humid. The living hull is a five-layer sandwich including the Aegis-strain fungus, Cladosporium sphaerospermum, which feeds on radiation through radiosynthesis. Below the city runs The Spine, feeding into The Crypt, where Oris runs the Cathedral, a quantum computer that flash-purifies water into structured EZ-water gel, the fourth phase of water, hexagonal and honey-viscous. In the EZ Aqua Core, aquaponics systems grow fish and plants together.

The asset emerges from this fusion: a floating cultivation pod that drifts through the aquaponics pools, pulsing with bioluminescence, tended by cultivators who read its rhythms like farmers once read the weather.

Mira Solheim exists in the registry as a medic, fifty-six, working out of The Pearl, with background in maintenance at Root Weave Junction 17-Alpha. Here I imagine her in an earlier chapter, when she served as a cultivator in EZ Aqua Core. A woman who remembers the pools.

The pulse-floater is roughly the size of a human head, a translucent membrane balloon filled with EZ-gel that breathes a slow rhythm matching the algae's diurnal cycle. Inside, a pellet of processed Cladosporium biomass feeds on trace radiation and glows faint amber. Cultivators nudge them with long poles, herding drifts like shepherds.

Logbook entry:

Logbook kept by Mira Solheim, Cultivator Third Tier, EZ Aqua Core, Section Three.

Shift Fourteen, Year 812 After Launch.

The pods are restless tonight. I feel them through the pole before I see them, a thrumming that travels up the wood and into the bones of my wrist, irregular, almost playful. Oris must have shifted something in the flow, because the whole pool leans west, and twenty of my charges have broken formation to chase whatever nutrient plume she sent us. I do not mind. They know what they want better than I do. I am only the one who feeds them and listens.

I started in Root Weave, third shift, deep below the city where the air tastes of recycled copper and old sweat. I was twenty-two and certain I would die in those tunnels. Then my sister Lin grew sick from something the doctors could not name, and I began to study the water that falls through The Spine and rises as gel in The Crypt. I learned to read EZ-water by its light, its temperature, the way it holds a seed in suspension. Lin recovered. I never went back to the tunnels.

Now I tend these pods, which are not built but grown, seeded from Aegis-strain cultures mixed with EZ-gel from Oris's Cathedral. They float because the gel inside is lighter than the pool water, and they pulse because the fungus pellet is always eating, always breathing, always turning something invisible into something warm. When I press my palm against the membrane, I can feel the day cycle of the algae outside. The pod knows. The pod adjusts its glow so I can see by it.

Tonight the new attendant, a boy named Pavel who came down from Metropolis thinking he would be bored, asked me why the pods matter. We have protein vats. We have mycelium farms. Why bother with something that drifts?

I told him the vats do not sing.

The pulse-floater remains essential to Ombra Prime's food chain, a quiet hymn of light and biology in the pools below The Pearl.
