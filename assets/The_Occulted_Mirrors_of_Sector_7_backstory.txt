The Occulted Mirrors of Sector 7 are not built but grown in the outer maintenance ring, obsidian alloy backed by living Aegis capillaries through which Vitalis Plasma flows. The surface stays mirror-clear until a face nears within thirty centimeters, then darkens to ink while the capillaries pulse amber, warm as hearth-light on a winter no one aboard has ever seen. The Orthodox call them Desecrations. The technicians on third shift call them quiet.

Captain-Surgeon Eliott Bramwell, fifty-two, has delivered seventeen children in zero gravity and read the dead more than the living. He passes the mirrors each evening from med-bay to quarters, and somewhere in the final kilometer he slows. The scanners confirm no data flows. Yet when he pauses, the light beneath the obsidian shifts in rhythm with his breath. Others see nothing. He sees a woman with his own eyes and jaw, wearing archivist robes from before the Archive had a name, looking back from a place that feels older than the ship.

LOGBOOK: Year 891, 03:17 ship-time. The mirror was where it always is, Junction 7-Alpha. My hand rose without permission. She stood there again, the woman, patient as someone watching a seed they planted decades ago begin to open. Her eyes were mine. Her jaw was mine. There was no recognition in her, only unhurried attention. I am a scientist. I have held fading pulses and signed death tallies without flinching. But tonight she moved, and the amber light followed, and I understood that whatever grows behind these mirrors is not waiting for permission. It is waiting for me. Tomorrow I will not mention it.
