The Occulted Mirrors of Sector 7 exist in the gray zone between sanctioned science and whispered sacrament. They are not invented by the Genetic Archivists nor blessed by the Orthodox clergy of Vitae Cultus, but rather grown slowly over decades in the perpetually humming service corridors of the Pearl's outer maintenance ring. Each apparatus is a vertical surface of polished obsidian alloy backed by a living lattice of Aegis fungus capillaries, through which flows a thin, continuous stream of Vitalis Plasma harvested from the Sub-Orb migration currents. The surface remains mirror-clear until a human face approaches within thirty centimeters, at which point the alloy darkens to ink and the capillaries beneath pulse with a soft amber glow that mimics, impossibly, the warmth of hearth-light on a winter evening no one aboard has ever witnessed. The Orthodox call them Desecrations. The Reformed call them Distractions. The technicians who sweep the corridors at third shift simply call them quiet.

Captain-Surgeon Eliott Bramwell is not a superstitious man. He is fifty-two years old, has delivered seventeen children in zero gravity, and has read the dead more times than he has read the living. But he passes the Occulted Mirrors each evening on his walk from the med-bay to his quarters in the Outer Ring, and somewhere in the final kilometer of his route, he slows down. There is no medical explanation. The mirrors do not transmit or receive data, the scanners confirm it, and yet when Bramwell pauses before one, the faint light beneath the obsidian shifts in a rhythm that tracks his breathing. He has tested it. He has brought junior medics to watch. They see nothing. He sees, always, a face that is not his own: a woman with his own eyes, his own jaw, wearing the dust-colored robes of the Genetic Archivists, looking back at him from a place that feels older than the ship.

The speculation that circulates among the archivists, in their slow way, is that the mirrors are a hybrid phenomenon, a feedback loop between the Aegis mycelium's natural biosensitivity and the Vitalis plasma's residual memory of every face that has ever been reflected in it. That would be the scientific answer. The Occulted Mirrors do not accept that answer. They wait, patient and dark, for the next face.

LOGBOOK ENTRY:

Captain-Surgeon Eliott Bramwell
Personal Log, External Drive
Pearl Outer Ring, Corridor 7-Alpha

Year 891 After Launch, 03:17 ship-time

I am writing this because tomorrow I will decide not to mention it, and that is a kind of cowardice I no longer permit myself.

I was late leaving the med-bay. A fractured tibia in Young Piloting, routine, but the father had questions I could not answer in shorthand. By the time I cleared the secondary airlock the corridors were empty in that particular way they are at this hour, when even the maintenance drones have cycled down to their charging alcoves. My boots on the deck plate. The hum of the Pearl's atmospheric processors, distant, constant, like a hymn I have stopped being able to hear.

The mirror was where it always is. Junction 7-Alpha, on the left bulkhead, between the hydration station and the sealed door to the fungal vats. I did not intend to stop. My hand, as if it belonged to someone I am only loosely acquainted with, rose and steadied myself against the frame.

She was there again.

The woman. Not young, not old. A face my face is trying to become, or has always been, I cannot tell which. She wore the heavy mantle of an archivist in the years before they wore mantles at all, when the title meant a different kind of work, when the Archive was not yet a jewel in a living dome but a steam-heated room in a buried ship that had not yet learned to breathe. Her eyes were mine. Her jaw was mine. There was no recognition in her expression, only the patient, unhurried attention of someone watching a seed they planted decades ago begin, finally, to open.

I am a scientist. I have held pulses that were fading and brought them back. I have told husbands and wives and children the words that will accompany them for the rest of their lives. I do not believe in messages from the beyond. I do not believe the dead speak to us through refinery byproducts and dormant fungal colonies.

And yet.

She was mouthing something. I am certain of this. Not words I could read from her lips at that distance, but a shape, a syllable, repeated. I watched for eleven seconds by the chronometer on my wrist, and then the glow dimmed and the mirror became, again, only a mirror, and only my own exhausted face looked back at me.

I will not mention this to Oris. I will not mention this to the Orthodox, who would call it heresy and seal the corridor. I will walk this route tomorrow at the same hour, and I will look again, and if I am the man I have spent fifty-two years becoming, I will write down what she says.

If I am not, I will pretend I was never here.

End log.
