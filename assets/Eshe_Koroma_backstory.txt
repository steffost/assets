I want to take you down. Past the green-gold blur of the biosphere, past the cultivation rings where water sings through pipes older than my grandmother. Down. Through the climate-controlled haze of the habitat ring, where a hundred thousand souls breathe and argue and sleep. Down further, into the Hangar's chrome and amber flicker. Then deeper still, into the Cathedral's territory — into the humming dark that belongs to Oris and her tireless children. That is where you will find what I have come to rely on, the thing the crews call the Pulsing Hoverer, though its formal designation is far less poetic.

The Hoverer was not designed in any drawing office. It was not decreed by MR Splendid or commissioned by Captain Attreus. It grew. Like most strange and useful things aboard Ombra Prime, it grew out of necessity and out of Oris's patient, ancient creativity. Down in the Gut, where maintenance crews traverse the hanging stalactite forests of the Root Weave, they kept dying. Falling. Misjudging the dark below. The Crypt's answer was the Hoverer — a disc of cultivated Aegis-mycelium, alive and damp, with a core of crystallized EZ-gel that pulses soft cyan in time with its wearer's heartbeat. It senses the abyss and resists it. It does not carry you so much as it refuses to let the void have you.

The mycelium shell metabolizes trace radiation leaking through every seam in our hull, feeding a small Levitas field compressed and localized. A little pocket of refusal. The disc levitates perhaps fifteen centimeters above whatever surface it detects, biased toward keeping its bonded wearer upright. The crystallized gel reads bioelectric signature and refuses to bond to anyone but its registered owner. You cannot steal a Hoverer. You cannot gift one. It knows you, or it is dead weight.

Oris distributes them sparingly, only to those who spend their shifts in the vertical dark. There are perhaps four hundred active Hoverers in the Gut at any given time, and twice as many stories about them.

LOGBOOK ENTRY:

Logbook kept by Eshe Koroma, Structural Maintenance Technician, Second Shift
The Root Weave, Junction 22-Theta, eastern catacomb
Year 1,041 After Launch

The Hoverer woke me this morning. That is the only way I can put it. I had fallen asleep against the corridor wall, helmet still on, lamp dimmed to its amber standby. Twelve hours in the dark does that to a person. You stop being a person and start being a sound. A breathing. A small warm thing that the abyss is politely ignoring.

But the Hoverer does not ignore. It pulses. It pulses against my chest where it is clipped, a slow cyan heartbeat that syncs to mine when I am well and races ahead when I am not. When I dream of falling — and one always dreams of falling, down here, where the dark below is so total it feels like a held breath — the gel at its center warms against my sternum. Not heat exactly. Recognition. A small biological insistence: you are not falling. You are held.

I woke to it pulsing faster than my own heart. My lamp, dead. The emergency strip along the catwalk, dead. Some flicker in a far conduit had killed the line, and I had been sleeping in true dark, true Root Weave dark, the kind you can taste. Old oil. Ozone. The cold exhalation of the ship's deep circulation.

I could not see my hands. I could only feel the Hoverer.

It pulled me upright. Not violently — gently, the way a parent lifts a child from a crib. The disc whirred softly as its micro-thrusters tilted, finding me, then steadying. I walked back to Junction 22-Theta with one hand on the wall and the other pressed to the cyan warmth over my heart. By the time I reached the lit corridor, my Hoverer had slowed to match my breathing. It knew I was safe. It had decided, and I believed it.

In the Gut, the Hoverer is not a tool. It is a covenant. You carry it, and it carries you, and neither of you mentions the abyss.
