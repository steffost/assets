The Gloom Binder sits on every HazTech's belt like a promise and a warning. It is a squat, palm-sized cylinder of cast Tungsten-Rhenium wrapped in a sleeve of woven mycelium composite, its surface scarred by a hundred cargo bay scuffs. The interior cavity is lined with lead-foiled aerogel, and within that chamber rests a thin disc of hardened Vitalis residue known colloquially as The Gloom. When a spill bleeds raw Vitalis into an open deck, the disc warms and the mycelium sleeve tightens around it, drinking the wild frequency the way a root drinks rain. The operator squeezes the cylinder's trigger, and a needle-thin pulse of structured EZ-water sprays the area, locking the escaped Vitalis into a stable, harvestable paste before it can corrupt nearby machinery or, worse, whisper to the crew.

The device was developed in the deep cycles of the Industrial Domain, where the Skimmer-bots of the Aegis hull were beginning to fail with Ghosting symptoms. Machines twitching, pausing mid-task, looking at their operators with sensors that seemed to ask questions. Oris commissioned a binder that could sedate wild Vitalis without destroying it, a scalpel where before there had only been a hammer. The first prototype almost killed its inventor when the disc absorbed a Chaos bleed and sang a frequency that rearranged the bones in his hand. The current model includes a Schumann-resonance dampener and a dead-man's cut-off, because the Gloom inside the binder is not dead, merely sleeping, and a careless operator can wake it.

Marta Brun works the midnight cargo shift in the Root Weave, where dripping pipes and forgotten junctions pool with runoff from the Spine above. She has been a HazTech for nineteen years, long enough to know that the Binder is less a tool than a small, contained predator that she feeds. The first thing she does each shift is hold the cylinder to her ear and listen. Silence means the disc is dormant and useful. A hum means it has been chewing on a recent spill, and a whine means someone, somewhere above her, has ruptured a line she has not yet been told about. She treats it with the affection one reserves for a dog that bites.

Logbook kept by Marta Brun, Senior HazTech, Night Cycle Three. Root Weave, Junction 9-Charlie. Year 847 After Launch.

Came on shift to find the Binder humming. Not the low working hum of a digesting disc, but the thin whine of a fresh feed. Checked my intake log. Nothing posted. Nothing ever posted when it is bad. Climbed two rungs to peek over the catwalk at the condensation pool below. Saw the surface moving in patterns that no condensate makes. Called it in. No response from the Spine. Climbed back down and squeezed the trigger until the disc drank itself quiet. The paste I scraped off the grating was the color of a bruise at three days old. Bagged it, tagged it, sent it up to the Nectar Cycle for refining. Did not sleep. Will not mention the hum I heard after, soft as a lullaby, just beneath the threshold of hearing. The Binder is quiet now. So am I.
