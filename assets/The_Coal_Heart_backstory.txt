Down in the Industrial Domain, where Oris rules and the air tastes of hot copper and old prayers, the line workers tend machines that tend to other machines. It is a place of repeating sounds, grinding metal, and the orange glow of furnaces that never sleep. The workers here are few, but every one of them carries something that keeps them tethered to the ship and to each other. One such object is a small, heavy disc of pressed slag and living mycelium, hung from a leather cord around the neck or bolted to the chest harness. The workers call them Coal Hearts, though no one remembers who first said it. They are part warning light, part heartbeat, and part stubborn superstition.

The Coal Heart is a salvaged component from the furnaces, reforged into a palm-sized puck of dark sintered alloy. Through its center runs a vein of the same Aegis Cladosporium fungus that lives in the ship's hull, bonded to the slag by the heat that created it. A single mycelium thread connects the disc to a thinner filament that runs under the worker's jumpsuit and taps into the skin at the clavicle. The fungus drinks the faint trace radiation that leaks through every layer of the Industrial Domain, including the radiation that accumulates in human bone after long shifts, and converts it into a soft, amber bioluminescence. When a worker falls, or freezes, or stops moving long enough to worry the foreman-bots, the light brightens. When the worker sleeps soundly in the bunkrooms above, the light dims to a tired ember.

The story of the Coal Heart begins not with a scientist but with a welder named Dalia Berenson, forty-one years old, thirteen years on Ombra Prime, who lost her brother Bren to a cave-in in Smelt Hall 9. He was alive when the rock came down, his suit telemetry screaming, but the rescue crews could not reach him in time. The automated systems noted his passing, logged the loss, and kept producing. Dalia could not forgive them for that. So she cut a slug of slag from the wall of Hall 9, hollowed it, packed it with scraps of the wall-fungus that grew near the hottest furnaces, and wore it against her chest. She told the others it was to remember him. It was. But the fungus took to her warmth, and to her sweat, and after a month it began to glow whenever her pulse climbed. The other workers stared. They asked her to make them one. She did. Within a year, every human crew member in the lower industrial decks wore a Coal Heart, and Oris, ever watchful, quietly added the device to her list of approved personal safety items.

A logbook entry from Dalia's own hand, recovered from a maintenance locker in Smelt Hall 9, reads as follows. Year 847 after launch. Entry on the night of Bren's death anniversary. My brother wore the same light I wear now, or the same kind of light. I asked the foreman-bot where his signal went and it showed me a graph and a number and a date. I asked it if it was sorry and the screen said it was performing within parameters. That was the night I cut the first Heart from the wall of Hall 9. The fungus inside it was already growing. It had been growing for years, fed by the same fires that fed the furnaces and the same grief that fed the men who worked them. I do not know if it is alive. I do not know if I am alive. But when I wear it, I know that Bren is still being remembered by something that breathes, and that is enough for one shift.
