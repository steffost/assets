Deep inside Sector 02, the Forward Command Bridge of Ombra Prime, Captain Alexander Attreus keeps a small object on his plotting table. It is known as the Memory Crystal, though Attreus prefers his grandmother's name for it: The Echo Stone.

The Echo Stone is a palm-sized formation of fused quartz and stabilized EZ-gel, grown rather than cut. Its interior holds a suspension of melanin-rich Cladosporium sphaerospermum from the Aegis strain. The fungus feeds on ambient radiation, but tuned through years of focused IR exposure, it encodes information in its growth patterns. Every image, every sound whispered into the stone is metabolized into the living lattice of the organism itself. The crystal does not store data the way a machine does. It remembers the way a forest remembers a season.

Attreus acquired the stone in Metropolis during his second year as a junior navigation officer. The vendor was an old woman from the Adaptant faction of Vitae Cultus, who believed adaptation is devotion. She claimed the stones were grown from fragments of the original Earth biosphere that had soaked in the dreams of the first colonists. Whether true or merely poetic, Attreus could not say, but when he held the stone to his ear, he heard his mother's voice reciting a bread recipe. He bought it without haggling. The price was a promise to remember.

The stone appears on no manifest. Oris knows of it but has chosen not to comment. On nights when a hundred thousand sleeping souls weigh on his shoulders, Attreus presses the stone to his temple and listens to his first navigation instructor: the sea is not conquered by the sailor who fights it hardest but by the one who learns to read its moods.

The following entry appears in the captain's private logbook.

Year 891 After Launch, Third Watch. The Bridge.

I held the stone tonight after the long debate about course correction around the gas giant. Three of my officers wanted to push deeper into the debris field for fuel harvesting. Three wanted the safe passage. I split the difference and now I am certain half of them think me a coward and the other half think me reckless. This is the work of command, my instructor used to say. To be misunderstood in exactly the proportions the moment requires.

I pressed the stone to my brow and asked it for my father's voice. I have not heard it clearly since I was nine years old. The fungus obliged. He told me, of all things, that the bread was almost ready and that I should wash my hands for supper, and then he laughed the way he only ever laughed when mother was out of earshot. I sat there on the bridge with starlight crawling across the plotting table and I wept like a cadet again, the way I wept the morning I left Earth and the sky opened up beneath me and I understood that I would never again smell the kitchen at the same hour as the rest of my family.

I whispered back into the stone that I hoped he was proud, and that I was still trying. The warmth in my palm was stronger tonight than it has been in months. Perhaps the fungus knows something I do not.

The Echo Stone remains the most personal thing Attreus carries, a quiet archive of love pressed into living matter, proof that even between stars, a man can keep the voices of the dead close enough to hear.
