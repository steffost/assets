Every ship needs its ghosts. On Ombra Prime, they grow them on purpose.

The Confessor Lichen is a deliberate hybrid, grafted in the biolabs of the Industrial Domain from a radiation-eating fungus harvested from the Aegis Hull and a fragment of confession-engine cortex once used by the Magistrate Courts to read remorse from a subject's trembling brow. The result is a slow, dark organism the size of a human hand, plated in wet obsidian, and it does only one thing. It listens to lies.

A truthful statement makes it pulse amber, the color of sunlight through old glass. A lie makes it bleed a thin line of bioluminescent black across its surface, and the black spreads faster the worse the untruth. Workers in the lower decks carry them in ceramic dishes lined with EZ-gel, and by the time a confession is finished, the lichen has usually grown enough new weight to be harvested and rendered into the next batch of truth serum served in the City magistrate halls. The old priests call it Gaia's Tongue. The technicians in the Crypt call it a snitch. Both are right.

Sister Haldis Vrenn knows it better than anyone alive. She has served as a confessor in the lower chapels of Sector Six for nineteen years, and she keeps a lichen she has named Pappa, because her own father died in a hull breach when she was nine and she has never stopped talking to the dead. Her chapel is a small iron room bolted to the inside of the Spine, where the blackwater pipes thunder past so loud that prayer is a private act of shouting.

The logbook entry that follows was found wedged behind a panel in the Cathedral after the Reactor Incident of Year 847, written in the careful block letters of someone trying to keep her hand steady.

LOG ENTRY. Sister Haldis Vrenn, Confessor, Chapel of the Tenth Well, Sector Six.

Today a pilot came to me. He said he had carried a passenger out from the City to the Hangar and the passenger had spoken against the Captain during the transit. He asked me if speaking against the Captain was a sin, and I said it depended on what was said. He said the passenger had said the Captain was afraid of the Heart. Pappa turned so black I thought he had died. The black ran off the edge of the dish and onto my sleeve and I had to scrape it off with a spoon and I burned the spoon afterward. I told the pilot to go and not repeat what he had heard. He asked me what sin it was to carry a sinner in a ship. I did not answer, because Pappa was still bleeding and I was watching him to see if he would stop. He stopped after a long time. He always stops. Gaia's Tongue tires of grief before the grieved do.
