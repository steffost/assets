The Threadrunner is not a vehicle in the classical sense. It is a personal transit apparatus grown rather than built, designed for the dense vertical labyrinth of the Metropolis where traditional vehicles would suffocate the streets and elevators cannot keep pace. Its chassis is a skeletal frame of woven mycelium composite, pale and fibrous as driftwood, with the organic feel of something that was once alive and chose to remain useful after death. The runner moves on six articulated limbs tipped with adhesive grippers, allowing it to walk up walls and cling to ceilings with the surety of a spider crossing silk. Steering is accomplished through a haptic collar worn around the rider's neck, which reads intent through subtle muscular contractions.

Among the residents of the Metropolis, the Threadrunner has earned the affectionate nickname "The Pulse."

LOGBOOK ENTRY:

Logbook of Dasha Mireles, Vertical Courier, Threadrunner Unit 47-C

Metropolis Sector 12, Transit Corridors

Day 1142, Local Reckoning

I almost did not take the job this morning. The message that came through my Nexus last night was not from my usual dispatcher but from someone I do not recognize. The sender's signature was a single black dot on a white field, which is the old courier shorthand for a job too important to refuse and too dangerous to discuss. I almost let it sit. I have a daughter who will turn nine next rotation, and I have been thinking lately that the walls of the Metropolis are very thin and the drop below them is very far and my hands are not as certain as they were when I was twenty. The message asked me to retrieve a sealed capsule from a deceased archivist's safe-deposit in Sector 9 and deliver it to an Oris Station in Sector 4 before the station closed for the calibration cycle. Twelve hours. I chose the Threadrunner.

Unit 47-C knows me. I have ridden it for nine years, since my daughter was a red and screaming thing and I was young enough to think that piloting a bio-frame across a six-hundred-meter fall was a reasonable thing to do for money. The unit's haptic collar is synced to my heart in the way that the old manuals describe — a soft amber throb against my spine that quickens when I quicken, slows when I slow, and sometimes, when I am frightened, seems to slow even before I know I am frightened.
